Digital Citizenship

What is Digital Citizenship?

Digital citizenship is the ability to navigate, evaluate, and engage with online environments responsibly, safely, ethically, and effectively. It encompasses the skills, etiquette, and critical thinking required to participate in a connected society.

What is Digital Law?

Digital Law is the legal framework governing the creation, use, distribution, and protection of electronic information, digital technology, and online activities. It establishes the legal rights, restrictions, and responsibilities of individuals, businesses, and governments in the digital world.

What is AUP?

An AUP (Acceptable Use Policy) is a formal set of rules and guidelines established by an organization such as a school, workplace, or internet service provider (ISP) defining how its computer networks, devices, and digital resources may be used.

What is Plagiarism?

Plagiarism is the act of presenting someone else's words, ideas, research, media, or intellectual work as your own without providing proper credit or citation.

It is considered a major violation of academic integrity, professional ethics, and intellectual honesty.

What is Academic Dishonesty?

Academic dishonesty is any action or attempted action that creates an unfair academic advantage for oneself or another student, violating the standards of integrity set by an educational institution.

It misrepresents a student's actual knowledge, skills, or efforts.

What are the rules for AI Generated Content?

The rules for AI-generated content (AIGC) vary based on context, but they generally pivot on disclosure, academic integrity, copyright, and platform policies.
